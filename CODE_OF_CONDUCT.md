# Code of Conduct

## Our promise

Callisto is an open project, and everyone is welcome to take part in it: asking questions, reporting bugs, suggesting ideas, improving the design or writing code. We want it to be a friendly and safe place for people of every background and every level of experience, whatever their age, gender, nationality, religion, disability or looks.

This Code of Conduct applies in every space of the project: issues, pull requests, comments, reviews, discussions and any other place where someone represents the project in public.

## What we expect

- **Be kind and patient.** Many contributors here are learning. Explain things, do not look down on people.
- **Be respectful when you disagree.** Argue about the idea, never about the person.
- **Give feedback that helps.** Say what to change and why. Take feedback on your own work as help, not as an attack.
- **Respect people's time.** The maintainers and contributors are volunteers. Wait for a reply before asking again, and do not demand that something is fixed by a certain date.
- **Take responsibility.** If you make a mistake, say so, fix it and move on.
- **Write so everyone can follow.** Not everyone speaks English as a first language, and that is fine. Keep messages short and clear, and use simple words when you can.

## What is not acceptable

- Insults, name-calling, personal attacks or put-downs, including "jokes" that make a person feel small
- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private, and following someone around the project to bother them
- Discriminatory, sexual or violent language or images
- Threats, or sharing someone's private information (address, phone number, email, photos) without their permission
- Trolling, spam, and posting off-topic or advertising content in issues and pull requests
- Pushing someone to claim, drop or finish an issue, or taking an issue that someone else has already claimed without asking them first
- Anything else that would reasonably make someone feel unwelcome or unsafe in a professional setting

## Reporting a problem

If you see or experience behaviour that breaks this Code of Conduct, please tell us. Do **not** answer in the same thread, it usually makes things worse.

- Contact the maintainer, **[@Hasalawa](https://github.com/Hasalawa)**, privately, using the contact details on their GitHub profile.
- For a single comment, issue or pull request, you can also use GitHub's **Report content** option (the `...` menu on the comment). This tells GitHub as well.

Please include links to what happened and any context that helps us understand it. Reports are read only by the maintainer, and we keep the name of the person who reports private. Reporting in good faith will never count against you.

## What happens next

The maintainer reads every report, looks at what happened and replies to the person who reported it, normally within 7 days. Depending on how serious it is, the response may be:

1. **A private reminder**, with an explanation of what was not acceptable and why.
2. **A public warning**, or removal of the comment, issue or pull request.
3. **A temporary ban** from the project, for example blocking someone from commenting.
4. **A permanent ban** from the project, for serious or repeated behaviour.

The maintainer may edit or remove any comment, commit, issue or pull request that does not follow this Code of Conduct. If the report is about the maintainer, please report it to GitHub using **Report content** or at <https://support.github.com/contact/report-abuse>.

## Scope and changes

This Code of Conduct applies to everyone who takes part in the project, including the maintainer. It can be changed over time, and the latest version on the `main` branch is the one that counts.

For how to contribute, see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). To report a security problem, please follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.
